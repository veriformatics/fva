### Q4

**ExtL1B: no remaining engine-selection obligation.** `Cut/ExtB.extL1B` and `segA` exist at `:229,249`; their old-stepper proof explicitly retains the overflow case (`:82–107`) and exact bound exit. `Cut/SegA.lean:128–154` permits arbitrary initial `acc < 2^31`, not just SegC's 2 MiB-derived safe accumulator. **SegB: composition and observations after writes.** Its near-end return uses snapshot `move`, whereas its far path deliberately overcopies `8 * chunks` bytes and then observes the *original source's* offset bytes (`Seg.lean:52–97`). The proof must retain room slack, caller-local state and source separation through S2/S3 contracts. S5 demonstrated related joins and offset preservation, not this exact near-return/general path. C's explicit local-vector normal form is useful here; duplicating mutually inconsistent vector/update conventions would spend the observed saving again on adapters. Keep named code tails and symbolic copy memories at block boundaries.

**SegD: highest remaining combined risk.** Its offset<8 prologue reads two tables, writes four zero-valued bytes then ordered copies, and uses wrapped negative `dec64` entries 4294967295/4294967292 (`Seg.lean:183–215`). Those are precisely the large-literal patterns that overwhelmed C's backward-copy attempts before symbolic BitVec bases repaired them (`spike-c/report.md:189`). C's prior `cp4_until` also required explicit bounds for loads after writes (`SC/S1.lean:58–68`; report `:188`). Cutpoints must distinguish error before any write from capacity error *after* the 8-byte prologue (`Seg.lean:237–244`), preserve the table area, and carry exact near/far copy memory into the next head. Read-only SegC does not establish the cost of that combination.

**I3 refinement:** memory-evolving induction is already demonstrated, not wholly new: C's `cp1_until` generalizes `st mem d s`, reads from the updated memory, and proves ascending `fwdCopy` without a non-overlap premise (`SC/S1.lean:11–44`). I3 has different decoder locals/control and still needs its own execution link. More importantly, its *successful near-end* tail has at most two iterations (also stated in `docs/contracts/U19-decoder-spike.md:19,59`). From `Seg.lean:194–205,238–244`: `cpy + 5 ≤ oend`; `lim = oend − 7`; the tail starts at `max(o' + 8, lim)`; `oend ≥ 8`. Therefore `cpy − start ≤ 2`. This is a deduction from the frozen definitions, not a new Lean-checked lemma here. Prove the arithmetic lemma, then check whether 0/1/2 byte blocks are simpler than a new general induction; retain exact evolving-memory semantics and footprint. The unfamiliar risk is the surrounding prologue/wildcopy/tail composition, not an unbounded new byte-loop mechanism.

All risks concern proof completion/resources, not permission to weaken the statements. `Top.lean:193–229` consumes exact `SegB`/`SegD` hypotheses for all R1–R3; stalled coverage must follow the user's milestone stop rule.
